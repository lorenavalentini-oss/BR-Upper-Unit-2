Warm-up: Imagine you are preparing to speak with a professional contact about an investment process. Your goal is to sound accurate, tactful and curious—not to explain ETFs.

Verified Avantis context: Avantis describes an investment approach grounded in financial science, using market prices and company financial information to assess expected returns. Discuss: What information do you usually need to clarify before a financial meeting? Which questions could sound too direct? What could you say instead?

3 · Grammar toolkit

01 · INDIRECT QUESTIONS

Ask for information diplomatically

Polite opener + question word / if / whether + subject + verb

Direct: Where does the team assess risk?

Indirect: Could you tell me where the team assesses risk?

Yes / No: Could you confirm whether the figures have changed?

✓ Statement word order inside the question.
✗ Could you tell me where does the team assess risk?

Useful openers: Could you clarify…? · Do you happen to know…? · I'd like to find out… · I wonder…

02 · NEGATIVE QUESTIONS

Check assumptions or make suggestions

Negative auxiliary + subject + main verb?

Checking: Haven't we received the updated figures yet?

Suggesting: Why don't we review the assumptions?

Tone matters. “Why haven't you sent it?” may sound accusatory. “Could you let me know when it might be available?” sounds more diplomatic.
03 · QUESTION TAGS

Confirm information and invite agreement

Positive statement → negative tag
Negative statement → positive tag
The report is ready, isn't it?
They haven't confirmed it, have they?
The figures changed, didn't they?
We should review the spread, shouldn't we?
Let's check the details, shall we?
Everyone received the report, didn't they?
↗ Rising intonation = genuine question. ↘ Falling intonation = expecting agreement.
